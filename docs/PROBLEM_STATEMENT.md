## Status

🔬 Hypothesis — Not Yet Validated

---

## Current Hypothesis

Ordinary internet users may struggle to determine whether digital content they encounter online is safe.

Potential examples include:

- Links received through messaging applications
- Social-media links
- Email links
- Login pages
- Download links
- Shortened URLs
- Scam messages
- Potentially malicious files

---

## Target Population

Initial research population:

**College students and young internet users in India.**

This population is being used for early research because it is accessible to the founder and has significant exposure to digital services.

It is not yet confirmed as Trinity's final target market.

---

## Suspected User Problem

A user receives potentially dangerous digital content.

The user must decide:

> "Can I trust this?"

They may not have enough information to make that decision confidently.

---

## Current User Behavior

To be determined through research.

We need to understand:

- Whether users investigate suspicious content
- How they investigate it
- Which tools they use
- Whether they trust browser warnings
- Whether they ignore warnings
- Whether they ask other people
- Whether they simply take the risk
- What causes them to click anyway

---

## Existing Solutions

Research required.

Potential categories:

- Browser security
- Search-engine protection
- Antivirus
- URL scanners
- Threat intelligence services
- Security extensions
- User education

---

## Potential Gap

Not yet validated.

One possibility is:

> Existing security systems may detect certain threats effectively but may not always provide users with sufficient context, explanation, or protection at the precise moment a user is deciding whether to interact with suspicious content.

This must be tested rather than assumed.
