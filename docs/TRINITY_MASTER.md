# 🔱 Project Trinity — Master Document

## 1. Mission

Build accessible cybersecurity technology that helps ordinary people make safer decisions and protect themselves from real-world digital threats.

---

## 2. Vision

Trinity aims to become an intelligent cybersecurity layer that can detect, explain, and help prevent digital threats before they cause harm.

Long-term possibilities include:

- Real-time web protection
- Malicious URL detection
- File and download protection
- AI-powered cybersecurity assistance
- Threat intelligence
- Security automation
- Specialized cybersecurity AI models
- Community-driven threat intelligence
- Consumer cybersecurity
- Business cybersecurity

These are long-term possibilities, not current commitments.

---

## 3. Current Stage

**Stage:** Problem Discovery & Validation

**Status:** 🔬 Research

Trinity is currently determining which specific cybersecurity problem should be solved first.

No major product-development decisions should be made until sufficient evidence has been collected.

---

## 4. Current Hypothesis

We believe ordinary internet users may have difficulty determining whether digital content they encounter online is safe.

This may be particularly relevant to:

- Suspicious links
- Phishing websites
- Scam messages
- Fake login pages
- Download links
- Malicious files
- Social-engineering attacks

We believe existing security tools may not always provide users with sufficiently understandable, timely, and actionable information at the moment they need to make a security decision.

This hypothesis must be validated through:

- User interviews
- User behavior research
- Competitive analysis
- Cybersecurity research
- Threat intelligence
- Technical feasibility analysis

---

## 5. Initial Research Population

### Initial population

College students and young internet users in India.

### Why?

This population is:

- Accessible to the founder
- Highly exposed to digital services
- Frequently exposed to links and online communication
- Diverse in cybersecurity knowledge
- Suitable for early qualitative research

This is a research population, not yet a confirmed target market.

---

## 6. Core Problem

Not yet validated.

Current research question:

> At what point do ordinary users struggle to identify or respond to digital security threats, and what intervention could meaningfully reduce that risk?

---

## 7. Research Principles

1. Evidence over assumptions.
2. User behavior over stated preferences.
3. Problem before solution.
4. Security before convenience.
5. Privacy by design.
6. Simplicity for users.
7. Avoid unnecessary data collection.
8. Do not assume AI is required for every problem.
9. Do not build features without validated user need.
10. Be willing to change the product direction based on evidence.

---

## 8. Competitor Research

Initial competitors and adjacent solutions to investigate:

- Google Safe Browsing
- Microsoft Defender / SmartScreen
- VirusTotal
- URLScan
- Browser security mechanisms
- Security browser extensions
- Consumer security products
- Indian cybersecurity/scam-protection products

The objective is not to prove Trinity is better.

The objective is to determine:

> What is already solved, what remains unsolved, and where a new product could provide meaningful value.

## 10. Product Hypothesis

To be determined after research.

Current possibility under investigation:

> Real-time cybersecurity assistance that evaluates potentially dangerous digital content and provides understandable, actionable warnings before the user is harmed.

This is not yet a product specification.

---

## 11. MVP

The MVP will be defined only after problem validation.

Potential areas under consideration:

- URL analysis
- Real-time web protection
- Threat intelligence
- Risk scoring
- Explainable security warnings
- Browser extension
- File analysis

No feature is currently committed.

---

## 12. AI Strategy

AI will be used only where it provides measurable value.

Potential applications:

- Threat classification
- Security explanation
- Cybersecurity assistance
- Threat intelligence analysis
- Pattern detection
- Security automation

A proprietary SLM will not be developed during the initial phase.

A specialized model should only be developed if evidence shows that it provides a meaningful advantage over existing models.

---

## 13. Data Strategy

Potential future sources:

- Public threat intelligence
- Licensed threat intelligence
- Security research
- User-reported threats
- Internally generated security datasets
- Experimentally generated datasets

Privacy-sensitive information must not be collected without a clear purpose, appropriate safeguards, and legal justification.

## 16. Major Risks

### Product Risk

The problem may not be significant enough to justify a new product.

### Competition Risk

Existing platforms may already solve the problem sufficiently.

### Detection Risk

Incorrect security decisions could damage user trust or cause harm.

### Privacy Risk

Security products may require access to sensitive information.

### AI Risk

AI systems can hallucinate and misclassify threats.

### Business Risk

Consumer cybersecurity can be difficult to monetize sustainably.

### Technical Risk

Real-time protection may require significantly more infrastructure than an initial prototype.

---

## 17. Current Non-Goals

Trinity will NOT currently attempt to build:

- A full antivirus
- A full enterprise SOC
- A complete SIEM
- A proprietary LLM/SLM
- Autonomous unrestricted security agents
- A full mobile security platform
- A complete cybersecurity ecosystem

These are future possibilities only if justified.

---

## 18. Current Questions

1. What exact cybersecurity problem should Trinity solve first?
2. Who experiences this problem most severely?
3. How frequently does it occur?
4. How do users currently solve it?
5. Why do existing solutions fail or create friction?
6. Would users actually change their behavior to use Trinity?
7. What can Trinity do substantially better?
8. Can the problem be solved technically?
9. Can it be solved without excessive data collection?
10. Can the product become economically sustainable?

---

## 19. Decision Rule

Trinity will not move into serious MVP development until there is sufficient evidence that:

1. A real problem exists.
2. A specific user group experiences it.
3. Existing solutions leave a meaningful gap.
4. Trinity can technically address that gap.
5. Users demonstrate interest in the proposed solution.
