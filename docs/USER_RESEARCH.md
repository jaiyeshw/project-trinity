# 🔱 Trinity — User Research

## Objective

Understand how ordinary users encounter, identify, and respond to cybersecurity threats.

The goal is to understand actual behavior rather than convince users that Trinity is necessary.

---

# Research Rules

- Do not pitch Trinity.
- Do not lead participants toward a preferred answer.
- Ask about real experiences.
- Ask follow-up questions.
- Record contradictions.
- Do not collect unnecessary personal information.
- Anonymize participants.
- Never store passwords, financial information, private messages, or other sensitive information.

---

# Research Target

Initial goal:

**30+ interviews**

Initial population:

**College students and young internet users in India.**

---

# Interview Questions

## Experience

1. Have you ever received a suspicious link?

2. Where did you receive it?

3. What did you do?

4. How did you decide whether it was safe?

5. Have you ever clicked a link that later turned out to be suspicious?

6. What happened?

## Security Tools

7. Do you use any antivirus or security software?

8. Have you ever seen a browser security warning?

9. What do you normally do when you see one?

10. Do you trust these warnings?

## Decision Making

11. What makes a link look suspicious to you?

12. What would make you trust a link?

13. If you are unsure about a link, who or what do you ask?

14. How much time would you normally spend checking something before clicking?

## Pain Points

15. What is the most frustrating thing about dealing with suspicious links or messages?

16. Have you ever ignored a security warning?

17. Why?

## Open Question

18. If you could change one thing about how the internet protects you from scams or malicious content, what would it be?
